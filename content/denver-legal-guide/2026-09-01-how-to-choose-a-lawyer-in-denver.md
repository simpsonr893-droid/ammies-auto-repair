# How to Choose the Right Lawyer in Denver: A Step-by-Step Guide

Finding the right attorney can feel overwhelming — especially when you're already dealing with a stressful legal situation. Denver has thousands of licensed attorneys, and the right one for your neighbor's business dispute is probably not the right one for your custody case. Here's a practical, step-by-step approach to narrowing the field.

## Step 1: Identify Your Practice Area

Law is highly specialized. Before you contact anyone, figure out which category your issue falls into:

- **Personal injury** — car accidents, slip-and-falls, dog bites, wrongful death
- **Family law** — divorce, child custody, child support, adoption
- **Criminal defense** — DUI, misdemeanors, felonies, record sealing
- **Estate planning** — wills, trusts, probate, powers of attorney
- **Real estate & landlord-tenant** — leases, evictions, closings, HOA disputes
- **Employment law** — wrongful termination, discrimination, wage claims
- **Business law** — formation, contracts, partnership disputes
- **Immigration** — visas, green cards, citizenship, removal defense

If your issue spans more than one area (for example, a divorce involving a family business), look for an attorney who handles the dominant issue and coordinates with specialists as needed.

## Step 2: Verify Credentials and Standing

Every attorney practicing in Colorado must be licensed by the Colorado Supreme Court. You can verify a lawyer's license status and any public discipline history through the Colorado Supreme Court's Office of Attorney Regulation Counsel. A quick search takes two minutes and can save you from a costly mistake.

Beyond licensure, look for:

- **Years of experience in your specific practice area** — not just years since law school
- **Trial experience**, if your matter could end up in court
- **Local familiarity** — an attorney who regularly appears in Denver District Court or Denver County Court knows the local judges, procedures, and timelines

## Step 3: Understand Fee Structures

Denver attorneys typically bill in one of four ways:

1. **Hourly rates** — common in family law, business, and litigation. Denver rates vary widely by experience and firm size.
2. **Flat fees** — common for estate planning documents, uncontested matters, and some criminal defense.
3. **Contingency fees** — standard in personal injury. The attorney takes a percentage of your recovery and you generally owe nothing if you don't win.
4. **Retainers** — an up-front deposit billed against as work is performed.

Always request a written fee agreement before work begins, and ask what costs (filing fees, expert witnesses, court reporters) are billed separately from attorney fees.

## Step 4: Prepare for the Consultation

Many Denver attorneys offer free or low-cost initial consultations. Get the most out of yours:

- Bring a **one-page timeline** of key events and dates
- Bring **relevant documents** — contracts, police reports, court papers, correspondence
- Ask: *Who will actually work on my case? What outcomes are realistic? What is your communication policy? What will this cost, best case and worst case?*

Pay attention to how the attorney communicates. If they can't explain your situation in plain English during the consultation, that won't improve after you've signed a fee agreement.

## Step 5: Trust the Fit, Not Just the Resume

Your attorney may be part of your life for months or years. Choose someone who listens, sets realistic expectations, and responds within a reasonable timeframe. A slightly less decorated lawyer who returns your calls is often a better choice than a big name who won't.

## When You Can't Afford a Lawyer

Denver residents with limited income have options, including Colorado Legal Services, law school clinics at the University of Denver and University of Colorado, and the Colorado Bar Association's modest means and pro bono programs. Courts also offer self-help centers (Sherlocks) for people representing themselves.
