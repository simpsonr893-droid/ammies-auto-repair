# What to Do After a Car Accident in Denver: 8 Steps That Protect Your Claim

Between I-25 congestion, snowy winters, and one of the fastest-growing metro populations in the country, Denver sees thousands of collisions every year. What you do in the minutes, days, and weeks after a crash can make or break an insurance claim. Here's what Colorado drivers should know.

## At the Scene

### 1. Stop, check for injuries, and call 911
Colorado law requires drivers involved in a crash to stop. If anyone is injured or vehicles are blocking traffic, call 911. Even for minor crashes, a police report creates an official record that insurers take seriously.

### 2. Move to safety, but don't leave
If vehicles are drivable, move them out of traffic lanes. Exchange names, contact information, driver's license numbers, license plates, and insurance details with the other driver.

### 3. Document everything
Photograph vehicle damage from multiple angles, the overall scene, skid marks, traffic signals, weather conditions, and any visible injuries. Get names and phone numbers of witnesses before they leave.

### 4. Watch what you say
Be polite and cooperative, but don't apologize or speculate about fault. A simple "I'm sorry" can be quoted back to you by an insurance adjuster later.

## In the Days That Follow

### 5. See a doctor — even if you feel fine
Adrenaline masks pain. Soft-tissue injuries, concussions, and whiplash often surface 24–72 hours after a crash. A prompt medical visit protects your health and creates a documented link between the crash and your injuries. Gaps in treatment are one of the most common reasons insurers reduce settlement offers.

### 6. Report the crash to your insurer
Most policies require prompt notice. Stick to the facts. You are generally not required to give a recorded statement to the *other* driver's insurance company — and it's usually wise to speak with an attorney before you do.

### 7. Preserve evidence and track expenses
Keep repair estimates, medical bills, prescription receipts, and records of missed work. Start a simple journal of symptoms and how the injuries affect daily life.

### 8. Know your deadlines
Colorado generally gives you **three years** to file a lawsuit arising from a motor vehicle accident. That may sound like plenty of time, but evidence disappears and witnesses move — and insurers negotiate more seriously when a claim is well-documented early.

## How Fault Works in Colorado

Colorado is an **at-fault** state with **modified comparative negligence**. That means:

- The driver who caused the crash (through their insurer) is responsible for the damages.
- If you are partially at fault, your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault.
- If you are found **50% or more at fault**, you recover nothing.

Because fault percentages directly control the money, insurance companies work hard to shift blame. Dashcam footage, witness statements, and the police report all matter.

## When to Call a Personal Injury Attorney

You may not need a lawyer for a minor fender-bender with no injuries. Consider a consultation when:

- Anyone was injured, even moderately
- Fault is disputed
- The insurer's offer doesn't cover your medical bills and lost wages
- The other driver was uninsured or underinsured
- The crash involved a commercial vehicle, rideshare, or government vehicle (special notice deadlines can apply)

Most Denver personal injury attorneys offer free consultations and work on contingency — meaning no fee unless you recover.
