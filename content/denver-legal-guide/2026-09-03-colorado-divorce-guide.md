# Divorce in Colorado: Timeline, Costs, and What to Expect in Denver Courts

Colorado calls divorce a "dissolution of marriage," and it is a **no-fault** state — the only ground is that the marriage is "irretrievably broken." Neither spouse needs to prove wrongdoing. Here's how the process actually works for Denver-area couples.

## The Basic Requirements

- **Residency:** At least one spouse must have been domiciled in Colorado for **91 days** before filing.
- **Waiting period:** A Colorado court cannot finalize a divorce until at least **91 days** after the petition is filed and served (or filed jointly). Most contested cases take considerably longer — often 6 to 12 months.
- **Where to file:** Denver residents file in Denver District Court; surrounding counties (Jefferson, Arapahoe, Adams, Douglas, Boulder) have their own district courts.

## The Process, Step by Step

1. **Petition.** One spouse (or both jointly) files a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage and pays the filing fee.
2. **Service.** If filed by one spouse, the other must be formally served.
3. **Initial Status Conference.** Colorado courts typically schedule this within about 42 days of filing to set deadlines.
4. **Financial disclosures.** Both spouses must exchange sworn financial statements and supporting documents — income, assets, debts, and expenses. Hiding assets can seriously backfire.
5. **Temporary orders (if needed).** The court can set temporary parenting time, child support, maintenance, and who stays in the home while the case is pending.
6. **Mediation.** Most Colorado courts require mediation before a contested hearing. Many cases settle here.
7. **Permanent orders hearing or decree.** If everything is agreed, the court can approve a separation agreement and parenting plan without a contested trial. Otherwise, a judge decides the disputed issues.

## How Property Is Divided

Colorado uses **equitable distribution** — marital property is divided fairly, which does not always mean 50/50. Generally:

- **Marital property** (acquired during the marriage) is divided, regardless of whose name is on the title.
- **Separate property** (owned before marriage, or received by gift or inheritance) usually stays with its owner — but the *increase in value* of separate property during the marriage is typically marital.

Retirement accounts, home equity, and businesses often require valuation experts in higher-asset cases.

## Parenting: Custody Is Now "Parental Responsibilities"

Colorado replaced the term "custody" with **allocation of parental responsibilities**, which covers two things:

- **Parenting time** — the schedule of when children are with each parent
- **Decision-making** — who decides major issues like education, health care, and religion

Courts decide based on the **best interests of the child**, considering the child's relationship with each parent, adjustment to home and school, and each parent's ability to encourage a relationship with the other parent. There is no automatic preference for mothers or fathers.

**Child support** is calculated under Colorado's statutory guidelines, based primarily on both parents' gross incomes, the number of overnights, and costs like health insurance and childcare.

## What About Spousal Maintenance (Alimony)?

Colorado has advisory maintenance guidelines based on the spouses' incomes and the length of the marriage. Maintenance is not automatic — courts first determine whether the requesting spouse actually needs it and whether the other spouse can pay.

## What Does Divorce Cost in Denver?

- **Uncontested with full agreement:** Court filing fees plus, if you use one, a flat-fee or limited-scope attorney — often the least expensive path.
- **Mediated cases:** Mediator fees are typically shared, and each spouse may hire counsel for review or coaching.
- **Fully contested litigation:** Costs escalate quickly with hourly attorney fees, experts, and multiple hearings. Contested custody or business valuation cases are the most expensive.

Many Denver family law attorneys offer unbundled ("limited scope") services — drafting documents or coaching you for a hearing — which can dramatically reduce costs for simpler cases.
