# Arrested for DUI in Colorado? Here's What Happens Next

A DUI arrest triggers **two separate cases at once**: a criminal case in court and an administrative case against your driver's license at the DMV. Understanding both — and their very different deadlines — is the single most important thing a newly charged driver can do.

## DUI vs. DWAI: Colorado's Two Impaired Driving Charges

Colorado is unusual in having two levels of impaired driving offenses:

- **DUI (Driving Under the Influence):** Typically charged when blood alcohol content (BAC) is **0.08 or higher**, or when impairment is substantial. A BAC of 0.08+ creates a permissible inference of DUI.
- **DWAI (Driving While Ability Impaired):** A lesser charge that can apply with a BAC as low as **0.05**, when alcohol or drugs affect you "to the slightest degree."

Both apply to alcohol, marijuana, prescription medication, and other drugs. For marijuana, a blood level of 5 nanograms of active THC permits an inference of impairment — but drivers can be charged below that level too.

## The Express Consent Law and Your License

By driving in Colorado, you've already agreed — under the **express consent law** — to take a blood or breath test if a police officer has reasonable grounds to believe you're impaired. Refusing the test has serious consequences:

- License revocation for **one year** for a first refusal (longer for repeats)
- Designation as a "persistent drunk driver," triggering ignition interlock and treatment requirements
- The refusal can be used against you in the criminal case

**Critical deadline:** If you fail or refuse a chemical test, your license revocation is handled by the DMV — and you have only a **short window (7 days when served with a revocation notice) to request a DMV hearing**. Miss it, and the revocation typically takes effect automatically, regardless of what happens in criminal court.

## Criminal Penalties: What's at Stake

Penalties depend on prior offenses and aggravating factors. In broad strokes, for adults 21+:

- **First DWAI:** Possible jail up to 180 days, fines, community service, and 8 DMV points
- **First DUI:** Possible jail up to 1 year, fines, community service, license revocation, and alcohol education/treatment
- **Repeat offenses:** Mandatory minimum jail time, longer interlock periods, and extended revocations
- **Fourth offense (lifetime):** Can be charged as a **felony** in Colorado

Aggravators — a high BAC (0.15+), a child in the car, an accident with injuries — increase exposure significantly. A BAC of 0.15 or more triggers persistent drunk driver consequences even on a first offense.

## What a DUI Defense Attorney Actually Does

Good defense work is not about tricks; it's about holding the state to its burden. Common areas of attack include:

- **The stop:** Did the officer have reasonable suspicion to pull you over?
- **The roadside tests:** Field sobriety tests are voluntary in Colorado and are affected by injuries, footwear, weather, and nerves.
- **The chemical test:** Breath machines require calibration and maintenance records; blood samples require proper handling and chain of custody.
- **The timeline:** Rising-BAC arguments can matter when there's a delay between driving and testing.

Even when the evidence is strong, counsel can often negotiate outcomes — such as a reduction from DUI to DWAI — that reduce points, fines, and long-term consequences.

## Practical Steps to Take This Week

1. **Calendar the DMV deadline immediately** and request your hearing if applicable.
2. **Write down everything you remember** about the stop while it's fresh.
3. **Don't discuss the case** on social media or with anyone but your lawyer.
4. **Get a consultation.** Many Denver DUI attorneys offer free case evaluations and flat fees for misdemeanor defense.
5. **Arrange transportation.** Plan for a period of restricted driving; early ignition interlock reinstatement is available in many cases.
