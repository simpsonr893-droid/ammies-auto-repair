# Denver Renters' Rights: Security Deposits, Repairs, and Eviction Rules Explained

With rents among the highest in the Mountain West, Denver tenants have more at stake than ever — and more legal protections than many realize. Colorado has significantly strengthened tenant law in recent years. Here's a practical overview of the rights every Denver renter should know.

## Your Home Must Be Habitable

Colorado's **warranty of habitability** requires landlords to keep rental homes fit to live in. A residence may be uninhabitable when it lacks things like:

- Functioning heat, running water, and hot water
- Working electrical, plumbing, and gas systems
- Weatherproofing and structural soundness
- Freedom from serious mold, pest infestations, or code violations
- Working locks on exterior doors and windows

**How to enforce it:** Notify your landlord of the problem **in writing** (keep a copy). Colorado law gives landlords a limited window to begin addressing habitability issues after written notice — and shorter timeframes when the condition is life- or health-threatening. If the landlord fails to act, remedies can include repair-and-deduct, lease termination, or a court action. Follow the statutory process carefully; simply withholding rent without following the rules can put you at risk of eviction.

## Security Deposits: Deadlines Matter

When you move out, your landlord must return your deposit or provide a **written, itemized statement** of deductions within **one month** (up to **60 days** if your lease says so).

Key points:

- **Normal wear and tear cannot be deducted.** Faded paint and worn carpet from ordinary use are the landlord's cost of doing business.
- If the landlord misses the deadline, they can forfeit the right to keep *any* of the deposit — and a tenant who sues over a **wrongful withholding** may recover **treble (triple) damages** plus attorney fees.
- Protect yourself with a documented move-in and move-out inspection: photos, video, and a dated condition checklist.

## Eviction: The Process Has Rules

A landlord cannot simply change the locks, shut off utilities, or remove your belongings — **"self-help" evictions are illegal in Colorado**. A lawful eviction requires:

1. **Written notice** — for nonpayment, Colorado requires a demand giving the tenant time to pay or leave before a case can be filed; other notice periods apply for lease violations and no-fault situations.
2. **A court case (forcible entry and detainer)** — you have the right to respond and appear.
3. **A judgment and writ** — only a sheriff can physically carry out an eviction, and only after a court orders it.

If you receive an eviction notice, **do not ignore it and do not move out reflexively**. Deadlines are short — get advice immediately. Tenants who show up to court with representation have dramatically better outcomes.

## Other Protections Worth Knowing

- **Late fees are capped and regulated** under Colorado law, and a landlord must generally wait a grace period after rent is due before charging one.
- **Retaliation is prohibited.** A landlord may not evict or raise rent because you reported code violations or exercised your legal rights.
- **Discrimination is illegal.** Federal and Colorado fair housing laws prohibit discrimination based on race, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin, and additional state-protected categories including source of income.
- **Lease terms can't waive your core rights.** Clauses purporting to waive the warranty of habitability or deposit protections are generally unenforceable.

## Where Denver Tenants Can Get Help

- **Colorado Legal Services** — free civil legal aid for income-qualified tenants
- **Denver's eviction legal defense resources** — the city has funded legal representation programs for tenants facing eviction
- **9to5 Colorado and Colorado Poverty Law Project** — tenant advocacy and clinics
- **Private landlord-tenant attorneys** — many offer flat-fee lease reviews and demand letters
