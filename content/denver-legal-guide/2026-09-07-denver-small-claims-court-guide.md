# How to Sue in Denver Small Claims Court: A Step-by-Step Guide

An unreturned security deposit. A contractor who took a deposit and vanished. A customer who never paid an invoice. For everyday money disputes, Colorado's **small claims court** is designed so ordinary people can resolve cases quickly, cheaply, and — usually — without lawyers.

## What Small Claims Court Can (and Can't) Do

Small claims is a division of Colorado's county courts. In Denver, that's the **Denver County Court**.

- **Dollar limit:** Up to **$7,500**. If you're owed more, you can waive the excess and stay in small claims, or file in county court (up to $25,000) or district court instead.
- **Typical cases:** Security deposits, unpaid loans or invoices, minor property damage, breach of simple contracts, defective goods or services.
- **What it can't do:** No evictions, no divorces, no defamation claims, and generally no cases seeking complex court orders rather than money.
- **Lawyers:** Attorneys are generally **not allowed** to represent parties in Colorado small claims court, with limited exceptions (for example, when the attorney is a party). This levels the playing field — but nothing stops you from *consulting* a lawyer beforehand to sharpen your case.

## Before You File: Send a Demand Letter

Judges expect you to have tried to resolve the dispute. Send a short, dated letter (or email) that states what happened, what you're owed, and a deadline to pay — typically 10 to 14 days. Demand letters resolve a surprising number of disputes, and the letter itself becomes evidence of your good faith.

## Filing Your Case, Step by Step

1. **Pick the right court.** Generally, file in the county where the defendant lives, works, or does business, or where the transaction occurred.
2. **Complete the Notice, Claim and Summons form** (available from Colorado Judicial Branch self-help resources and the clerk's office). Identify the defendant precisely — for a business, use its exact registered name, which you can look up through the Colorado Secretary of State.
3. **Pay the filing fee.** Fees are modest and scale with the claim amount; fee waivers are available for those who qualify.
4. **Serve the defendant.** The defendant must receive formal notice — typically by certified mail through the clerk, a private process server, or the sheriff. A case can't proceed without valid service.
5. **Wait for the response.** The defendant may pay, settle, file a counterclaim, or show up to contest. If they file a counterclaim over the small claims limit, the case can be moved to county court.

## Preparing for Your Hearing

Small claims hearings are short — often 15 to 30 minutes — so organization wins cases:

- **Bring three copies** of every document: contracts, texts, emails, invoices, receipts, photos, and your demand letter (one for you, the judge, and the other side).
- **Print things out.** Don't plan to show the judge your phone.
- **Prepare a two-minute summary:** what was agreed, what went wrong, what you're owed, and how you calculated it.
- **Bring witnesses** with firsthand knowledge, if you have them.
- **Be on time and address the magistrate or judge respectfully.** If you don't show up, your case is dismissed; if the defendant doesn't, you'll likely win by default.

## Winning Is Half the Battle: Collecting

A judgment is a piece of paper — the court does not collect it for you. If the losing party doesn't pay, Colorado law gives you tools:

- **Interrogatories or a debtor's exam** to force disclosure of assets and employment
- **Wage garnishment** through the debtor's employer
- **Bank garnishment** of the debtor's accounts
- **Judgment liens** against real property

Judgments in Colorado are valid for years and can be renewed, and they accrue interest. Persistence pays — literally.

## Is Small Claims Right for Your Dispute?

Small claims is ideal when the amount is within the limit, the facts are documentable, and the defendant is findable and collectible. If your dispute involves more money, complex legal issues, or a defendant likely to bring counsel to a higher court, a consultation with a Denver civil litigation attorney can help you choose the right forum before you file.
