# How to Choose the Right Personal Injury Lawyer in Denver (2026 Guide)

If you've been hurt in an accident, the lawyer you choose in the next few weeks may matter more than almost any other decision in your case. Denver has hundreds of personal injury attorneys — billboards on I-25, ads before every YouTube video — and from the outside they all promise the same thing. This guide gives you a practical method for telling them apart.

## Why your choice of lawyer actually matters

Insurance companies track law firms. They know which attorneys settle quickly to keep volume moving and which ones prepare every case as if it will see a courtroom. Firms with a reputation for trying cases routinely receive stronger settlement offers, because the insurer's alternative is an expensive trial. When you hire a lawyer, you're not just buying advice — you're buying that reputation.

## Five things to look for

**1. Real experience with your type of case.** "Personal injury" covers everything from fender-benders to catastrophic brain injuries. Ask directly: *How many cases like mine have you handled in the last three years, and how did they resolve?* A truck-crash case, a premises liability claim, and a medical negligence claim are handled very differently.

**2. Trial experience — not just settlements.** Most injury cases settle, and that's often good for the client. But ask when the attorney last took a case to a jury verdict. A lawyer who never tries cases negotiates from a weaker position.

**3. Who will actually work your file.** At some high-volume firms, the attorney on the billboard will never touch your case. Ask who your day-to-day contact will be, how often you'll get updates, and whether you'll have the handling attorney's direct contact information.

**4. Resources to front your case.** Serious injury cases require money — accident reconstructionists, treating-physician depositions, economists for lost-earnings calculations. Ask whether the firm advances case costs and how those costs are repaid from a settlement.

**5. A clean disciplinary record.** Colorado's attorney regulation office maintains public records of attorney discipline. A quick search of the lawyer's name takes two minutes and can save you from a serious mistake.

## Understanding contingency fees

Nearly all Denver personal injury lawyers work on contingency: they're paid a percentage of your recovery, and nothing if you lose. Typical percentages run around one-third of the settlement, often rising if the case goes into litigation or trial. Before signing, make sure the fee agreement answers three questions in writing:

- What percentage applies at each stage (pre-suit, litigation, trial, appeal)?
- Are case costs deducted before or after the fee percentage is calculated?
- What happens if you and the lawyer part ways mid-case?

There is no such thing as a stupid question about a fee agreement. A good lawyer will walk you through it patiently; a hesitant answer is information too.

## Red flags to walk away from

- **Guaranteed outcomes.** No ethical lawyer guarantees a result or promises a dollar figure at the first meeting.
- **Runners and solicitation.** If someone contacted you unprompted after your accident — at the hospital, by text, through a "friend of a friend" — that solicitation itself may be improper. Choose your own lawyer.
- **Pressure to sign today.** A legitimate firm will give you time to read the agreement and compare options.
- **No interest in your medical treatment.** Your health drives your case. A lawyer focused only on a quick settlement, before your injuries are fully understood, is not working for you.

## Don't wait too long

Colorado limits how long you have to file an injury lawsuit — generally two years for most injury claims, and three years for claims arising from motor vehicle accidents, with important exceptions in both directions (claims against government entities, for example, require a formal notice on a much shorter timeline). Evidence also degrades fast: surveillance footage gets overwritten, witnesses move, vehicles get repaired. Talking to a lawyer early costs nothing and protects everything.

## FAQ

**How much does a consultation cost?**
Almost every Denver personal injury firm offers free consultations. Bring your accident report, photos, insurance information, and medical records if you have them.

**Can I switch lawyers if I'm unhappy?**
Yes. Clients can change counsel, though the prior firm may claim a portion of the eventual fee for work performed. It's usually cleaner to choose carefully the first time.

**Will my case go to trial?**
Statistically, probably not — the large majority of injury claims settle. But hiring a lawyer who is *prepared* to try your case is exactly what makes a fair settlement more likely.
